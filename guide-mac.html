Install Seene on your Mac

About two minutes. Your Mac will show a warning the first time — that is expected, and the steps below walk you through it.

Why the warning? Seene is a private beta that hasn't been through Apple's public review (notarization) yet. The warning is about Apple's review status, not a problem found in the app.

You'll need

  • A Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or newer). Not sure? Apple menu → About This Mac → Chip.
  • About 6 GB of free disk space, and your Mac's administrator password (to install).
  • DaVinci Resolve Studio or the free version. Your synced project is built for you either way.

Setup

  1. Open the downloaded file and double-click the installer

    Open Seene_v2_Mac_Beta.dmg. Inside you'll find Seene_v2_Mac_Installer.pkg (the installer), this guide, and a plain-text copy of the steps. Double-click the installer.

    macOS says it can't verify the file. Click Done. Do not click “Move to Trash”.

  2. Allow it in System Settings

    Apple menu → System Settings → Privacy & Security. Scroll all the way down to the Security section. You'll see a message about the installer and a button — click Open Anyway, then enter your password or use Touch ID.

    Don't see Open Anyway? It only appears after step 1 and stays for about an hour. Double-click the installer again, then reopen Privacy & Security.

  3. Click through the installer

    Choose Continue, then Install, and enter your password when asked. When it says the installation was successful, click Close. An older Seene is removed for you.

  4. Open Seene from Applications

    Finder → Applications → Seene (or Launchpad). It should open without a second warning.

    If macOS still blocks the app, open Terminal (⌘+Space, type “Terminal”), paste this line, press Return, then open Seene again:

    xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Seene.app

    It only unlocks Seene, nothing else on your Mac.

  5. Agree to the beta agreement

    The first time, Seene shows a short confidentiality agreement. Enter your name and email, tick the box and click I Agree — Continue. You do this once on each Mac.

  6. Drop a card and let it work

    Click Choose card or folder (or drag your card in). Seene reads your slates, matches the audio and shows progress; Cancel is there if you want to stop. When it finishes it opens your new synced project in DaVinci Resolve.

    First time with Resolve: if macOS or Seene asks, open System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility, switch Seene on, then click Open in DaVinci Resolve again. If Resolve was already open, Seene may ask you to quit it (⌘Q) once and try again.

    Keep your Mac awake and plugged in during big cards, and please don't click around in Resolve while Seene is building your project.

If something goes wrong

No “Open Anyway” button

Redo step 1 (double-click the installer), then reopen Privacy & Security and scroll to the bottom. The button shows for about an hour after each attempt.

The installer says it's damaged or can't be opened

The download may be incomplete. Download it again and compare the checksum shown on the download page with your file's.

Seene is blocked after installing

Run the one-line Terminal command from step 4, then open Seene again.

A card seems stuck

Click Cancel and run it again. Big cards can take a while — if there's still no movement after a long time, tell us the card size and what the progress text says.

Seene asks to control Resolve, or to quit and reopen it

Switch Seene on in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility. The first time, Seene installs a small helper that Resolve only loads at startup, so quit Resolve completely (⌘Q) and click Open in DaVinci Resolve again. This is needed once.

Resolve doesn't open your project

Make sure DaVinci Resolve (Studio or Free) is installed and not showing a dialog box, then try again.
